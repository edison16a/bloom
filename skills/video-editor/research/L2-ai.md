# L2-ai: AI product launch and feature films (2023–2026)

25 official launch or feature videos from OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, ElevenLabs, Runway, Midjourney, Gamma, Granola, Lovable, Vercel v0, Replit, Cognition (Devin), Glean, Cursor, Notion, Linear, Pika and The Browser Company (Arc). All come from the brands' own channels, run 18 s to 3:54, and were uploaded between Oct 2023 and Mar 2026. All were measured with `tools/measure.py`. A local copy of it was used, changed only so that a subtitle HTTP 429 error does not abort the download (`--ignore-errors`). Harvey was searched for, but no official launch film turned up, so it was left out.

## Measurement caveats (read first)
- **`cuts_per_min` undercounts badly for motion-design films.** scdet (threshold 12) catches hard cuts only. Morphs, dissolves, pans across one continuous canvas and UI that slides in all count as "no cut". v0 (0 cuts), Devin (0), Linear (0), Cowork (3), Deep Research (1), Glean (4) and Cursor (1) all change layout every 2–4 s on the overview sheets. For those films I added an eyeballed **visual-state changes/min** taken from the overview sheet. It is a lower bound because the sheet samples only every 1–4 s, and it is marked "eyeballed".
- **`speech_share` looks broken for this batch.** The GPT-4o demo is wall-to-wall dialogue (subtitle estimate 255 wpm) but reads 0.12. Talking-head films read 0.2–0.38. Treat it as a relative signal only. `subtitle_wpm_estimate` is more trustworthy for "is there VO".
- **Chance level for `cuts_on_beat_share`** (±60 ms window) is about 0.12/beat-period, which is ≈0.16 at 80 BPM, ≈0.20 at 100 BPM and ≈0.26 at 130 BPM. A share near those numbers means *not* beat-cut.
- **BPM:** the search floor is 70 BPM. ChatGPT agent read exactly 70.0, so it may be clipped. Values of 70–80 may be half-time readings of 140–160 tracks. Beat strength stayed ≥1.56 in every video.
- `hits_per_min` counts every sharp transient. In beat-driven tracks (Cursor, Arc Max) the hits sit on the beat (hit-on-beat share 0.94 and 0.92), so there they mostly measure percussion, not SFX.

## 2. Per-video notes

**01 Say hello to GPT-4o (OpenAI, 2024).** Hook: a cold open with no title. A locked wide shot of a presenter with a phone sits beside a phone close-up insert, and the conversation is already underway. Structure: one continuous take, with a single cut at 17.5 s (dark B-roll of the phone in hand) and a near-silent gap at 18–19 s (−72 dB). No text animation and no logo until the end. Sound: the AI voice and the human voice carry everything. The 50 hits/min are speech plosives and laughs (inference). Flow: static. ## 3. Batch synthesis

### Typical ranges (n = 25)
| Metric | Median | IQR | Range | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 80 s | 55–127 s | 18–234 s | Most launch films are 45–130 s |
| Cuts/min (scdet) | 5.3 | 1.1–10.0 | 0–48.5 | Undercounts motion design; eyeballed state changes for those films run 11–34/min, typically ~19 |
| Median shot | 5.8 s | 2.9–32 s | 0.9–110 s | Bimodal: sizzles 0.9–2.9 s, interview films 4–6 s, canvas films "one shot" |
| BPM | 97 | 78–113 | 70–135 | Fast-energy films sit at 106–135 (Granola, Lovable, Cursor, Notion, Deep Research, ElevenLabs ×2, Glean, Arc) |
| Beat strength | 3.1 | 2.1–4.2 | 1.6–11.1 | All tracks have a clear pulse |
| LUFS | −16.7 | −21.6 to −15.2 | −32.3 to −9.0 | Sizzles −9 to −15; VO films about −16; dev-tool "quiet" films −26 to −32 |
| Hits/min | 62 | 50–83 | 5–137 | Kinetic UI films 80–137; pure footage sizzle (Runway) 5 |
| % cuts with hit | 0.20 | 0.16–0.33 | 0–1.0 | Only Pika (0.59) and Arc (0.54) put a hit on most cuts |

Clusters:
- **A. Model sizzle reel** (Sora 2, Runway, Midjourney, Pika): 13–48 cuts/min, music only, loud (−9 to −16 LUFS), no VO.
- **B. Kinetic-type plus UI motion film** (Lovable 2.0, ElevenLabs ×2, Comet, Deep Research, v0, Notion, Cursor, Cowork, Glean, Arc): few hard cuts, but a new visual state every 2–4 s, 100–135 BPM, 60–137 hits/min.
- **C. Founder or engineer doc film** (ChatGPT agent, Claude Code, Opus 4.5, Gamma, Granola, Devin, Replit, Lovable Cloud): speech-led, 4–21 cuts/min, music at −16 to −26 LUFS under the VO.

### How pacing changes across the video
- Two patterns dominate. **Accelerate** (Runway 20→40; Lovable 2.0 6→18; Granola 5.5→11; Lovable Cloud 4→8.5; Sora 2 12→25 in Q3) or **front-load the hook** (Pika 75→35; Gamma 20→1; Comet 17→0). Nobody starts slow and stays slow, except Replit and Devin, the two least-crafted or most "webinar" films.
- A **final breath** before the logo is universal. The last shot or card runs 2–4× the median (Sora 2 final shot 8.6 s; Runway cloud-sea end 5+ s; Midjourney a 5.2 s hold). Mean energy in Q4 is ≥1 dB below Q3 in 19 of 25 films and ≥3 dB below in 10 (up to −7.8 dB), because the music fades out under the end card.
- Text films use a **0.2–0.4 s per word** reveal rate, then hold the full line for about 1–1.5 s before it is replaced. Nothing sits static for more than about 2 s without a camera drift, scroll or element entering.

### How cuts relate to the beat
- The measured on-beat share is near chance for most films (median 0.20). **Most launch films do NOT cut on the beat.** They cut on speech or on visual motion.
- The exceptions are deliberate. Midjourney cuts **every bar** (2.6 s = 4 beats at 92 BPM, 0.61 on-beat), Sora 2 gets 0.54 (≈3× chance) and Arc Max 0.46. When a brand beat-cuts, it does it on the bar or the half-bar, not every beat.
- In the fast type films the *beat* is carried by the **text and UI events**, not by cuts: a word, chip or click lands per beat while the "camera" never cuts (Lovable, Cursor, Notion; inference from hits on the beat at 0.28–0.94 while cuts are few).

### SFX density and placement
- The kinetic UI films run 80–137 transients/min, about 1.5–2.3 per second. With the visuals showing a cursor click, a keystroke or an element popping at that rate, the hits are most likely UI foley: clicks, key taps, soft pops and whooshes (inference, since the audio cannot be heard). Cowork has 93 hits/min at only −32 LUFS, which means the UI ticks are mixed *forward* of a very soft bed.
- Pure footage sizzles use almost no SFX (Runway 5/min) and let the generated clips' own audio or the music carry them. Sora 2 uses native per-shot sound instead of a music-only mix.
- Hits on cuts: 20% median. Big "impact + cut" moments are reserved for title-card slams (Pika 59%, Arc 54%). Practical rule: put an SFX on every *element entrance or interaction*, and on only about a fifth of the cuts.
- End-card **stinger lift**: +11 to +32 dB jumps at the logo (Comet +32.5 dB at 65 s, Gamma +14.5 at 162 s, Lovable Cloud +11.8 at 107 s, Claude Code +18.5 at 185 s after a deliberate 4-s drop).

### Text animation conventions
- **Word-by-word reveal** is the default (Cowork, Comet, Lovable, Deep Research, ElevenLabs, Linear, v0). Each word fades from about 0–30% opacity, often with a blur-to-sharp and a 10–20 px rise, staggered 0.15–0.25 s per word (≈4–6 frames at 24 fps). The line completes in 0.6–1.2 s.
- **Character typing** with a caret is used for prompts and taglines (Cursor about 40 chars/s; ChatGPT agent and Notion prompts typed at giant 3–4× scale; Granola and Pika wordmarks typed with a caret).
- **One emphasis word** gets a style change: italic serif inside a sans line (Deep Research), a gradient fill (ElevenLabs "AI agents that can speak", Lovable), or red (Gamma "BILLION-DOLLAR").
- **Scale contrast**: one word huge (cropped at the frame edges) that shrinks to its resting size in about 0.4–0.5 s (Lovable "kno|", Gamma "THE NEXT"); bold condensed caps for slam cards (Pika).
- **Vertical roll swaps**: a word or list item rolls up to the next item like a slot machine (Comet list, v0 placeholder carousel, Lovable "Cloud→AI", ElevenLabs language cycling, Linear spec). About 0.2–0.4 s per step.
- Placement: centre-frame, single line, plenty of empty space. The font is a neutral grotesk (most), or a brand serif (Anthropic, Comet, Deep Research emphasis). Lower thirds are only for real people (name / title / company, small, bottom left, fade).
- Captions in talking-head films: one sentence per box, bottom centre, small, dark rounded plate (Opus 4.5, Granola). No word-pop captions anywhere in this category.

### Flow and direction rules
- **Pick one travel direction and hold it.** ElevenLabs Productions spends 24 of its 66 s moving ←, Lovable 2.0 40 of 80 s ←, and Glean pans ← and ↑ along its node graph. New cards enter from the right and old ones exit left, so the eye reads the film like a line of text.
- **Vertical (↑) means "progress/list"**: terminals scrolling (Claude Code, 44 s of ↑), checklists building (Cowork), list rolls (Comet), chat bubbles stacking (ElevenLabs Conversational).
- **Z+ push-in means "focus on a detail"**, and **Z− pull-back means "reveal the product frame"** (Deep Research and Devin both open UI full-screen and then pull back to a framed window).
- **Motion-matched cuts in sizzles**: Runway chains forward-moving FPV shots so the push continues across each cut. Sora 2 links shots by subject action.
- **Graphic match transitions**: the period dot becomes an iris (Comet); a toggle pill morphs into the logo (Cowork); a line expands into a pill that becomes the prompt field (v0); the glyph becomes the wordmark (Opus 4.5, Lovable Cloud).
- The camera re-centres on the **newest element** every time. UI is never shown at 1:1 full screen for long: it is scaled 1.5–4× onto the active region, or inset on a coloured or gradient matte with a shadow or glow border (Claude Code coral, Notion blue, Lovable and Gamma glows, Deep Research gradient border).

### The 10 most reusable moves (recipes)
Frame counts assume 30 fps. Easing is my reading of the sheets (inference): mostly expo or cubic ease-out on entrances, ease-in on exits.

1. **Word-stagger headline.** Each word: opacity 0→1, blur 8→0 px, y +16→0 px over 10 frames, ease-out (cubic-bezier 0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1). Stagger 5–6 frames per word. Hold the full line 30–40 frames. Exit is all words together: opacity →0 and blur →6 px over 8 frames, or a blur-dissolve into the next line. SFX: none per word, or a soft tick on the last word; a low swell under the line. (Cowork, Comet, ElevenLabs, Linear, Deep Research.)
2. **Giant-type to resting size.** The key word types in at 3–4× scale with a caret, overflowing or cropped by the frame (2–3 chars per frame), then scales down to 1× over 12–15 frames with an expo ease-out while the rest of the sentence types on. SFX: key clicks per char at −20 dB, a whoosh on the scale-down. (Lovable 2.0, Gamma "THE NEXT", Notion "Creat|".)
3. **Emphasis-word swap.** In a sans line, set one word in italic serif or a gradient fill. Reveal the plain words first and the emphasis word 4–6 frames later with a slightly longer fade (12 frames). (Deep Research, ElevenLabs, Lovable.)
4. **Slot-machine roll.** Mask a line. The current word moves y 0→−100% while the next moves +100%→0 over 6–8 frames, ease-in-out, with a 1-frame vertical motion blur. Chain 3–6 items, one per beat. SFX: a short tick per roll. (Comet list, v0 placeholders, Lovable "Cloud→AI", ElevenLabs languages.)
5. **Dot-to-iris match transition.** The final punctuation dot, or a UI dot, scales from 1× to a frame-filling circle over 12–15 frames (expo-in), the frame becomes that colour, and the next scene opens from a shrinking circle or from a small element in the same position. SFX: a whoosh rising into a soft thump at full cover. (Comet; Pika uses the reverse circle wipe for a title card in about 9 frames.)
6. **Pill/line morph into UI.** A 1 px line draws across (8 frames) → grows vertically into a rounded pill holding the headline (10 frames, overshoot about 5%) → shrinks into the real prompt field (12 frames) → the caret starts typing. SFX: a soft "swish" on grow, a click when it settles. (v0; Cowork "Chat → Cowork" pill.)
7. **Full-screen to framed pull-back.** Open the UI cropped to fill the frame, hold 20–30 frames while text populates, then scale 1.0→0.8 over 18–24 frames (cubic ease-in-out). This reveals the window border, a 1–2 px gradient glow and the background matte. SFX: a low "air" whoosh. (Deep Research, Devin, Lovable glow frames.)
8. **Cursor-led UI beat.** Every 18–24 frames, one new UI event happens: the cursor glides to a target (8–12 frames, ease-in-out), clicks (a 2-frame scale 0.95 on the button), and the result element springs in from 10–20 px below with an opacity fade (8 frames, a slight overshoot). The camera reframes so the newest element sits centre, with a 2–3× push. SFX: a click on press plus a soft pop on the entrance. These make up the 80–137 hits/min. (Cowork, Notion, Lovable, ElevenLabs.)
9. **Bar-lock sizzle cut.** For model or footage montages, cut every 4 beats (2.6 s at 92 BPM, 2.0 s at 120 BPM). Use 2-beat cuts in the build section and an 8-beat hold for the hero shot. Choose clips whose internal motion continues the previous shot's direction (forward push → forward push). Music only, no SFX on cuts. Accelerate the cut rate about 2× from the first to the last quarter, then end on one 5–8 s hold. (Midjourney, Runway, Sora 2.)
10. **Slam title-card interjections.** Between demo shots, insert 12–30 frame black cards with bold condensed caps, one phrase fragment per card ("AND EVEN A" / "CANVAS?"). Cut in on a hit (an impact or riser end), with no fade. Optionally a 6–9 frame circle wipe on the first card. SFX: a sub-boom or snare on each card. (Pika; Arc uses calmer 36-frame blue cards with white sans.)

Bonus logo-outro pattern used by almost all 25: drop the music 3–6 dB about 2–4 s before the end, then have the glyph appear and the wordmark slide out from it (12–18 frames). The URL appears small beneath it, then hold 1–2 s and cut or fade to black. The visual lift of the logo comes with a +10 to +30 dB stinger relative to the dip before it.

### Amateur tells that the pros avoid
- **Raw full-screen screen recordings at 1:1** with small unreadable UI. Pros crop, scale 1.5–4× or inset on a matte; Replit is the one film that shows long raw recordings with a PiP, and it reads as a webinar.
- **Static holds over 2 s with nothing moving.** Even "slow" films (Linear, Claude Code) keep a drift, scroll or typing going.
- **Cutting on every beat** in a demo film. Pros cut on sentences (doc films) or on the bar (sizzles) and put the per-beat energy into element entrances.
- **SFX on every cut / whoosh spam.** Only 20% of cuts carry a hit. SFX belong to *interactions* (clicks, typing, pops).
- **Mixed motion directions** (cards entering from random sides). Pros hold one axis per section.
- **Many fonts and effects.** Every film uses one family (plus an optional emphasis style), one background system (gradient mesh, solid matte or dark field) and one accent colour.
- **Word-pop karaoke captions, emoji or sticker graphics:** absent from all 25.
- **A front half that is slower than the back half without a hook.** Every film shows either the product or a bold claim within 3 s. Runway's 10 s disclaimer is the lone exception, and it is deliberate framing.
- **Flat loudness through the logo.** Pros dip before the logo and land a stinger on it.
