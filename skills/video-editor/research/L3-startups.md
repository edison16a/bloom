## 3. Batch synthesis (n = 25)

### Measurement caveats
- **Cut counts undercount this genre.** UI-led launch films move through animated panels, overlays and single-take camera moves, not hard cuts. measure.py retries at scdet threshold 5 when cuts/min is under 6. After the retry, 03 Lance (2.1), 05 Wildcard (5.5), 09 Comena (2.8), 10 VibeFlow (5.2), 13 Cluely (2.1) and 19 Morgen (1.1) are still low, and the sheets confirm these are real continuous styles. For these films, hits/min and the sheets are the better pace signal.
- 23 Anvil's "+135.6 dB lift" is an artefact: the energy array has a digital-silence value of -180 dB.
- 01 Palmier's median shot (0.08 s) is skewed by its 3 s strobe, which contains 25 of its 40 cuts.
- 18, 19, 23 and 24 are low-craft or demo-style uploads. They are kept as contrast and drag the medians down.
- cuts_on_beat is 0 for many videos where the beat is weak or the cuts are few. Treat it as low-confidence.

### How pacing changes across the video
- Cuts/min by quarter: medians 11.1 / 6.1 / 8.1 / 4.4, means 16.6 / 8.2 / 9.3 / 14.6. The first quarter is the busiest in 44% of videos.
- The dominant shape is a **cold-open burst, then a slower demo, then a final lift**:
  - Bursts: sieve quarter 1 = 97 cuts/min, Artificial Societies 54, Anvil 44.
  - Final lifts: Palmier quarter 4 = 190, the "Introducing" showcase 47.
  - Single-take films (Lance, Wildcard, Tenera) stay flat on cuts and carry their energy in animation density instead.
- Best practice seen: chaos to calm. sieve's quarters 97/19/8/0 tie the edit rate to the story (problem = frantic, product = calm and confident).
- The Palmier pattern is a slow, comedic setup (shots of 3-7 s) that pays off with a 3 s strobe climax under one fixed caption. The burst works because of the slow build before it.

### How cuts relate to the beat
- Only about 14% of cuts land on the beat grid. The pros cut on speech phrases and on UI-event completion (a click lands, a panel settles), not on bars.
- The beat is felt through **animation landings** instead: the hits/min (median 53) are mostly UI pops, chip pops and counter ticks.
- Big structural moments sit on **music lifts** (all from energy_db_per_s):
  - +27.5 dB at 6 s in 02, at the Create click and reveal.
  - +19 dB at 27 s in 09 Comena, as the product demo starts.
  - +16 dB at 2 s and +14.8 dB at 32 s in 14 Cluely, the second on the "$15 Million" reveal.
  - +33 dB at 61 s in 20 Cascade, music re-entering after a silence beat at the story's resolution.
  - +19.7 dB at 1 s in 25 sieve and +19.8 dB at 1 s in 12 WithAI: music slams in on frame 1 to 30 of the cold open.
- Rule (inferred): put the turn ("Introducing X", "Let's see it in action", the money number) on a music lift, usually after 0.3-1 s of near-silence.

### SFX density and placement
- Measured transients: median 53/min (IQR 43-67). Voice-led films still carry about 50-67/min (Unveil: 51-67), so SFX are present even under VO.
- Placement inferred from hits plus visuals:
  1. UI element pop-ins (cards, chips, stat bubbles): a soft tick or pop on the settle frame.
  2. Cursor clicks: a click sound on every click (Sim, Comena, VibeFlow, Weave).
  3. Counter ticks during number roll-ups (Sim launch "3,600 to 5,000+", PlexTrac 45 to 76).
  4. A whoosh on whip pans and panel slides (Wildcard, Lance's exit whip).
  5. Glitch or static bursts on chaos montages (sieve, Knowlify).
  6. A boom or impact on the logo or money reveal.
- 40-70% of cuts carry a hit in the UI-led films (VibeFlow 70%, Comena 50%, Wildcard 40%, TrueMile 42%, Palmier 40%). Cinematic films (Cluely $15M, Cascade, Knowlify) use almost none on cuts (0-10%) and let the score carry the energy.

### Text animation conventions
1. **Word-by-word reveal synced to speech** (Tenera, TrueMile, Wildcard, Lamb Labs).
   - Each word fades and de-blurs in over about 3-5 frames, one word per spoken word.
   - The text sits in the negative space beside the speaker, never over the face, and sometimes splits around the head.
   - The line exits as a unit with fade, blur and a small downward drift over about 4-6 frames.
2. **Accent keyword colour.** One word per line takes the brand accent: orange (TrueMile, Lamb Labs), blue gradient (Sim, Dash, Mbodi uses red), or blue on micro-type (Neuron). Every other word stays white or black.
3. **Font classes:**
   - Clean grotesk (Inter-like) for SaaS.
   - Heavy condensed caps for punchy or parody films (Lamb Labs, Knowlify, PlexTrac).
   - Italic serif for cinematic subtitles (02).
   - Mono or typewriter for terminal or archival looks (Artificial Societies, sieve, Mbodi caret).
   - Tiny wide-tracked caps for 3D or hardware (Neuron).
4. **Stacked one-word-per-line columns** (WithAI "Smaller / funds / just / can't / match"), each line popping in as it is spoken.
5. **Typewriter with caret** on plain backgrounds for problem statements (Comena, Mbodi, Dash prompt bars).
6. **Big-number stat cards:** a huge number with a small label beneath. Glass card over the founder (TrueMile, Tenera) or full-screen (Comena "17%"-style, PlexTrac bubbles). Numbers count up over about 1-1.5 s with ease-out.
7. **Captions:** small boxed or plain white sentence captions bottom-centre (Lance, VibeFlow, WithAI, Cluely, Cascade). Hormozi-style condensed caps only in the most YouTube-native piece (Lamb Labs).
8. **Story text blocks, left-aligned, appending clause by clause** for cinematic narratives (Cluely $15M lower-left; Cascade top-left "That's Gary. / His AI support agent... / It cost $400k").
9. **End cards:** logo mark pops, then the wordmark types or wipes on, then a tagline with one accent word, then a URL or CTA in a pill (Unveil, Hinnacle, Comena). Black or white, always minimal.

### Flow and direction rules
- The dominant global flow is ← (12 of 25 videos), i.e. content moving left. This is consistent with "forward" reading: panels whip or slide toward the left and the camera trucks right (inference from flow plus sheets: Wildcard's whip-left, Comena's 10 s run of ←, VibeFlow's tilted UI pans).
- Founder footage is locked off or steadicam (40-96% static). The motion budget goes to the UI and text, not the camera. Unveil's Lance is the exception, with a steadicam push-in walk-and-talk.
- UI is shown at a 3D tilt of about 20-35 degrees while travelling, then flattened to front-on when the viewer must read it (VibeFlow, WithAI's task reel, Cluely's overlay punch-in, Anvil's retro screens).
- Exit direction matches entry: Lance's founder walks out of frame right, the camera whips right, and the logo builds. Cards that rise from below leave upward.
- Zooms are motivated by the cursor: auto-zoom toward the click target, ease-in-out, with motion blur (Weave, Sim). Punch-in scale on the UI is about 1.3-2x.

### Human-to-UI transition patterns (the user's focus)
How the 18 videos with people on camera get from the person to the product, in order of how often they appear and how well they work:
1. **Hard cut to a branded neutral canvas, then the UI materialises.**
   - Seen in Wildcard (warm grey), Sim Series A (blue vignette), VibeFlow (navy plus cyan glow), WithAI (off-white with logo), TrueMile (cream).
   - The canvas is empty for 2-6 frames, then the first UI element fades and de-blurs up or expands from a pill (Sim: pill to chip in about 5 frames).
   - It is the cleanest and most common pattern, especially in agency work (Unveil, Hinnacle, Office of Tomorrow).
2. **UI composited onto the human shot first, then full-screen.**
   - Tenera, TrueMile and Wildcard put glass cards, stat cards and app icons beside the founder before any full-screen UI.
   - The viewer meets the product as an object in the founder's space, which softens the cut.
   - Tenera saves full-screen UI for an "Introducing [logo]" white card at about 60% through.
3. **Screen in the shot, then the screen fills the frame (a motivated cut).**
   - Palmier: rack-focus from a branded bottle to the monitor behind, then a hard cut to the full-screen dock.
   - TrueMile: over-the-shoulder on a dispatcher's spreadsheet.
   - Dash: an over-the-shoulder laptop shot, then the UI, then hands typing, then a reaction shot, repeating like shot/reverse-shot.
   - Cascade: a monitor in the shot shows the real dashboard.
   - Knowlify: fire inside a CRT monitor, matched to a white room with a CRT that boots up.
4. **Continuous camera glide into a UI world.** Lance moves from a walk-and-talk lobby into a dark-teal 3D space where cards float and the camera keeps travelling. This takes the most craft and has the most flow.
5. **Picture-in-picture shrink.** Weave: the founder's full-frame webcam shrinks to a rounded corner bubble while the app takes over. Anvil and Morgen keep a circle-masked founder on screen the whole time. These are functional and low-energy.
6. **HUD overlay on the person.** Cascade types the product's evaluation checklist as translucent text over the protagonist at his desk. Mbodi overlays dashed detection boxes on the robot's table. The product appears inside the live-action world.
7. **Title-card bridge.** Cluely dissolves (3-4 frames) to black cards with italic text and an icon. Comena and VibeFlow use one-line text cards ("Let's see it in action") as the hinge. Hold 1-1.5 s with a slow scale-down, then cut to the first UI frame.
- **Return to the human:** most films go back to the founder for the close (Wildcard, Tenera, TrueMile, Sim A, WithAI, Lance, Cluely) and end with the logo over the founder or a separate end card.
- **Rhythm:** human and UI alternate every 5-15 s in founder-led films. Pure demos without a person, longer than about 30 s, are where the energy drops.
