# Film shapes

Choose one. Say which in the plan.

## A. Argument, then product (Hue, Artificial Societies, 0.email)
`real world moments` (10 to 30 s) then `turn` (silence) then `product` (15 to 30 s) then `end`.
1. Three to five real clips or posts that prove the problem. Each has one idea and 1.5 to 4 s on screen.
2. White words follow the speech, or two short lines at the left sum it up.
3. One line that turns: "Until now." or "Just simulate it." Cut to silence 0.4 to 1.5 s.
4. Logo builds out of something in the film. Music enters.
5. Product: three moments only. Start with the one that shows the promise being kept.
6. End on one sentence, then logo and button.

## B. Plain product (Ghostship)
`sentence` 3 s, `logo` 2 s, `UI in use` 15 to 25 s, `end`. The interface starts tilted in 3D, settles flat, then every move is a real click.

## C. Person first (Vendo, YouLearn, hybrids)
Person for the first lines, with overlays from the production skill, then one product block (12 to 18 s) on a soft brand field with small captions, then the person again for the close. Enter and leave the block with a fast vertical blur or a plain cut. Use the block once.

## D. Hardware or place (Open Actuation, Central Blue)
Real macro footage of the object, one huge white line that shrinks, silence, a person with a plain name, real factory footage, screens filmed with a camera or captured, logo.

## E. Story (Cascade, Atlog)
Needs real actors or footage from the user. Three lines of white text in empty space, music that lives inside the scene, the product as one plain text overlay, logo, line and URL on black, each alone.

## Intro choices
A direct sentence on black, an archive clip with its sound, a real post, a macro shot of the product, or the person. Never a logo, a title card, or a rhetorical "What if".

## Endings
Logo alone for 1.5 to 2.5 s. Then maybe the URL or one sentence. No list of features, no phone mockup, no confetti.

## Text budget
A 35 s film has 25 to 45 words on screen. If it has more, the words are doing the product's job.
